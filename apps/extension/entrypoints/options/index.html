Screenshots and display

Off unless you turn it on. When on, and a tab you are reading is mostly an image, a pasted screenshot or a canvas app, Carat takes one picture of that tab while it is in front and keeps it in session memory for at most three minutes, two pictures at most. When you switch away, the picture goes to the smart model, which writes down the text it can see plus a few facts it works out from the picture (dates resolved to the calendar, places, addresses, prices); the picture is deleted and only that text is kept, for 30 minutes like everything else. No picture is ever taken of a page Carat has offered to fill, of a page with a password field, or of a page on the denylist.

A short note when a chip is accepted, a softer one when Esc turns it down, and a rising pair when an action arms for its second Tab. Synthesized in the page at the moment you press the key; nothing is downloaded and nothing plays on its own. On by default. Pause for a moment in a field and Carat writes the rest of the line in grey after the caret. Tab takes it, any other key drops it, Esc drops it and stays out of that field until you change what is in it. Never in a password, card or code field, and never in an empty one: there Tab still belongs to the action chip. On by default. The accessibility tree is what a screen reader hears: the roles, names, values and states Chrome itself computed, frames included. Reading it means attaching Chrome's debugger to the tab, so Chrome puts a yellow "Chrome is being debugged by software" bar across the top of that window until Carat lets go, which it does after a minute of not reading the tab, and whenever you close it or navigate somewhere Carat may not read. Dismissing the bar, or opening DevTools, takes the debugger away; Carat then reads that tab's DOM instead until you next switch to it, and says so in the debug line. Choose "The page's DOM" to never see the bar; Carat then builds the outline itself, which reads closed components and cross-origin frames less well. A small line in the bottom-left corner of every page: whether Carat is running there and which model answers. Off by default.

Nothing Carat reads is written to disk. Context lives in session storage and is gone when the browser closes.
