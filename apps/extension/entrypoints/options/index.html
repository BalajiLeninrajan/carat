Carat settings

Carat reads the page as Chrome's accessibility tree, asks one model what you are most likely to do next, and offers it. Tab accepts, Esc dismisses, Ctrl+→ takes one word of ghost text. Something that cannot be undone wants Tab twice.

Model

What Carat offers

A chip beside the control Carat thinks you want next. Tab presses it, fills it, picks the option, or reads on down the page. Pause in a field and Carat writes the rest of the line in grey after the caret. Tab takes it, Ctrl+→ takes one word, Esc drops it. Never in a password, card or one-time-code field. When you leave a page, Carat asks the model to note the facts on it you might act on elsewhere, keeps them for an hour in session memory, and includes them in later prompts. The page's visible text is sent to the API to do this. Off by default. Skipped on blocklisted sites and pages with a password field.

Clipboard

What you copy on a page is always a note: Carat sees the copy without asking for anything, and keeps the text for an hour like everything else it reads. Nothing is kept from a copy made in a password, card or one-time-code field, from a page showing a password field, or from a blocklisted site, and text shaped like a password, a key or a card number is dropped whatever field it came from.

Off unless you turn it on, and it saves itself the moment you do. When on, Carat also reads what you last copied anywhere else on this computer, including other apps and other Chrome profiles. Chrome asks you for the permission when you tick the box and Carat gives it back when you untick it, along with everything that permission produced. It reads at most once every five seconds, only when you switch tabs or a page loads, never while the tab in front is blocklisted or showing a password field, and keeps only a hash of the last text between reads.

Display

A short note when a chip is accepted, a softer one on Esc, and a rising pair when an action arms for its second Tab. Synthesized in the page at the moment you press the key. A small pill in the bottom-left corner of every page: whether Carat is running there and which model answers. Off by default.

Carat holds a chrome.debugger session on the tab it is reading, which is why Chrome shows the yellow "being debugged" bar. Dismissing that bar pauses Carat on that tab; the toolbar icon resumes it. Nothing Carat reads is written to disk: the timeline and the notes live in session memory and are gone when the browser closes.
